# Thesis mode

## Plan one argument across chapters

Use the degree, discipline, thesis type, and local requirements that are known and relevant to the requested output. A provisional chapter map can proceed before the institution or submission format is chosen. For an institution-bound draft, check the current rules for manuscript format, inclusion of published work, author contribution statements, reuse permissions, and permitted and disclosed AI assistance. Where local limits affect drafting, provide chapter planning, editorial feedback, or revision of the author's text within those limits.

Write a one-sentence thesis answer and map the central question to chapter questions. For each chapter, state what it establishes, what it inherits, and which uncertainty the next chapter resolves. Use conceptual or evidentiary dependency to choose an order. This may differ from publication chronology.

| Thesis component | What it must accomplish |
| --- | --- |
| Introduction | Motivate the central puzzle, articulate the thesis answer, and explain how the chapters develop it |
| Literature and conceptual framework | Locate the precise disagreement or gap, define durable terms, and justify the framework used across chapters |
| Shared methods or research design | Explain choices and assumptions that recur, how chapters relate methodologically, and where their designs diverge |
| Contribution chapters | Give each chapter a question, evidence, limitations, and a closing step in the larger argument |
| General discussion | Reconcile similarities, contradictions, assumptions, and boundaries across chapters and explain what their combination establishes |
| Conclusion | Return to the central question, state the contribution at the right level of confidence, and identify questions that follow from unresolved evidence |

For a thesis built from papers, inventory their publication status, authorship, rights, and overlap. Respect the integrity of any required reprints. Where permitted, consolidate repeated background and notation, reconcile updated numbers and terminology, identify the candidate's contribution, and situate unpublished or negative evidence in the thesis discussion. Verify all attributions, permissions and disclosure requirements from the target institution.

## Use precise, composed prose

Put the intellectual progression in the language. Give sentences concrete subjects, keep terminology stable, and use paragraph openings to orient the reader to a question, inference, or dependency. Vary pacing where a dense derivation or methods passage needs an interpretive sentence. Let the opening chapter establish why the problem deserves sustained attention through a specific phenomenon, unresolved contradiction, or consequential boundary.

A chapter bridge can read, “Chapter 2 isolates the error under controlled shifts. Chapter 3 changes the deployment setting to test whether the same failure persists when the shift is unobserved.” Ground each bridge in the actual studies. Preserve intellectual history and publication context where either affects interpretation.

A table that maps aims to chapters, results, and limitations can orient an examiner. Use a thesis-wide conceptual diagram when it makes cross-chapter relationships legible. Reconcile figure numbering and notation across reproduced chapters using a note where reproduction rules require the originals.
