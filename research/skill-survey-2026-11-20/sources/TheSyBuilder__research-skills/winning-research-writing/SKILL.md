# The Winning Research Writing Skill

## Establish what the work earns

Read the user's draft, notes, results, code, proofs, figures, and target requirements as available. Determine the deliverable, research question, closest comparison, strongest supported claim, and material uncertainty. Identify the lead contribution by asking what finding, theorem, artifact, or intervention would still justify the work if its other contributions were removed. For hybrid projects, route the primary narrative through that contribution and give each secondary claim the evidence its own type requires.

State a working interpretation briefly when it changes the draft. Ask about a missing claim, dataset, or result only if the answer would materially change the work and cannot be recovered from the supplied materials. With partial evidence, deliver the requested draft as far as the evidence supports and mark exact author inputs needed, such as `[confirm whether splits are by patient]`. Keep unresolved claims out of clean submission copy.

## Draft around the evidence

- Map each material claim to its nearest result, proof, analysis, artifact, or primary citation. Record the population or formal setting, conditions, comparator, effect or conclusion, and main limitation. Keep the map internal unless the user requests it or needs to resolve gaps. Verify numbers, references, and figure-to-text consistency at their sources.
- Set the paper's promise in a sentence a domain reader can test. Put the research problem, specific gap, and necessary context where a reader first needs them. Lead result paragraphs with a scoped finding or question, then show the evidence and explain what it establishes. Give methods enough detail to reproduce the central argument. Organize by evidence dependencies and venue requirements.
- Choose a title and abstract that expose the actual contribution. When grounded, include the setting, closest meaningful comparator, and result with units or the theorem with its material assumptions. Keep secondary results visible in the appropriate sections. Define new notation once and use stable names across sections, captions, and appendices.
- Match verb strength to the design. A measured association supports an association, an ablation supports a comparison under its conditions, and a theorem supports its stated regime. Preserve meaningful negative results, implementation details, subgroup limits, and uncertainty. Source citations and numerical values from records. Mark unresolved details for the author and keep claims at the level supported by the existing work.
- Give each figure a question to answer. Keep the most decisive evidence close to the claim. For figure plans, specify the data needed, panels, marks, scales, comparisons, and caption claim. If data are missing, provide a labeled plan or schematic. Generate result plots from the real data with editable, reproducible source when the user requests figures and the data permit it.

## Run a reader pass before delivery

Read the contribution as a skeptical specialist would. Check whether the closest comparison is fair, the main inference survives its strongest obvious rival explanation, and the content lets a reader reproduce or check it. For a draft against reviewer feedback, map each actionable concern to a manuscript change, a supported explanation, or a clearly identified research gap. Audit numbers, citations, and declarations against their records, in a fresh context when one is available. Recheck the edited prose against the original claims, conditions, citations, numbers, and exceptions. Deliver the format and scope the user asked for. If a consequential gap remains, name the missing evidence and its effect on the draft.
